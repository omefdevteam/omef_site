Impact Philosophy

Impact is More Than Mere Activity

We do not see impact as mere activity. That's a big change: Healthier ecosystems. Stronger communities. More of a possibility. Better able to deal with what’s coming. We are transparent about what is verified, what is targeted, and what remains aspiration.

Measurement Discipline

How We Count Our Numbers

We use four labels consistently, so no one has to guess what a figure means:

Verified Progress

Achieved & Audited

An achieved result, backed by an identified source and reporting period, with the verifier named where independent verification applies.

Approved Target

Formally Endorsed

A formally endorsed future result, with a target date, baseline, owner, and a funded or defined delivery pathway.

Long-Term Ambition

Directional Scale

A directional aspiration that communicates scale without implying it's already committed or achieved.

External Reference Point

Comparative Context

Evidence from another organisation or comparable model, shown for context. Never counted as OMEF impact.

Holistic Evaluation

Four Dimensions of Sustainable Change

Our framework links environmental, social, educational, and institutional impacts across four interdependent spheres:

Nature

Land, water, biodiversity, ecosystem resilience. Safeguarding watersheds, recovering degraded soils, and bolstering living infrastructure.

People

Livelihoods, wellbeing, participation, dignity, and opportunity. Ensuring community members thrive through ecological restoration.

Future

Learning, digital capability, ethical leadership, intergenerational benefit. Empowering youth to lead and prosper in digital economies.

Systems

Institutions, methodologies, partnerships, public policy, long-term stewardship. Structural anchors that ensure sustainability holds.

Across all four: gender and inclusion, community rights and participation, safeguards and do-no-harm practice, local ownership, transparent evidence, and a real habit of learning and adapting.

Community Voices

Stories of Impact

Numbers give us scale. Stories teach us what that means. Our impact stories tell the stories of people as decision-makers, practitioners, learners, and leaders—with real context, credit to the partners involved, and care not to let one person stand in for an entire community.
