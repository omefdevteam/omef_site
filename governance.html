Section 1 · Document Purpose

Purpose of this Public Overview

This document provides a high-level description of the governance and organisational architecture of Our Mother Earth Foundation (“OMEF”). Its purpose is to help communities, partners, supporters, institutions, and members of the public understand how the Foundation is structured and how responsibility is broadly allocated.

It is a public-facing overview. It does not reproduce OMEF's detailed constitutional, operational, financial, or internal governance arrangements, which remain protected under constitutional documents and applicable law.

FOUNDATION MOTTO People. Nature. Future.
Section 2 · Foundation Tenets

Six Core Governance Principles

OMEF’s governance is designed to protect the Foundation’s public-interest mission and to support responsible, transparent, and effective delivery.

Principle 01

Mission Protection

All decisions and programmatic actions should strictly support the Foundation's public-interest purpose and long-term mandate for people and nature.

Principle 02

Responsible Stewardship

The Foundation’s resources—financial, human, ecological, and institutional—must be managed responsibly, prudently, and solely for their intended purposes.

Principle 03

Clear Accountability

Responsibilities must be allocated unambiguously between non-executive governance oversight and executive operational implementation functions.

Principle 04

Partnership

OMEF works collaboratively with local communities and institutional, public, academic, philanthropic, traditional leadership, and private-sector partners.

Principle 05

Integrity and Transparency

OMEF communicates openly about its work, impact, and resource stewardship, subject to legitimate legal privilege, confidentiality, and data protection.

Principle 06

Long-term Thinking

Governance structures support enduring initiatives capable of creating lasting intergenerational benefit far beyond individual donor or project cycles.

Sections 4 & 5 · Strategic Leadership

The Board & The Chair

Defining clear high-level oversight roles and explicit non-executive governance boundaries.

Section 4 · Strategic Oversight

The Board of Directors

The Board provides overall governance and strategic oversight of OMEF. Its role includes, at a high level:

  • Protecting the Foundation’s mission and public-interest purpose.
  • Overseeing strategy and organisational direction.
  • Overseeing responsible stewardship of the Foundation’s resources.
  • Providing appropriate oversight of programmes, partnerships, and institutional development.
  • Supporting accountability and transparency across all Foundation operations.
  • Overseeing the performance of executive leadership.
Operational Boundary: The Board does not manage every operational aspect of the Foundation. Day-to-day implementation is delegated to executive leadership within the Foundation’s governing framework.
Section 5 · Board Governance

The Chair of the Board

The Chair provides leadership to the Board and supports effective governance. Key functions include:

  • Providing leadership and cohesion to the Board of Directors.
  • Ensuring that Board discussions remain strictly focused on the Foundation's mission, strategic responsibilities, and public-interest purpose.
  • Promoting a culture of open debate, high integrity, and independent stewardship.
  • Facilitating constructive relationships between the Board and executive leadership.
Governance Boundary: The Chair does not replace the collective responsibilities of the Board or the executive functions of management.
Section 6 · Operational Execution

Executive Leadership

Translating Foundation strategy into programmatic reality through dedicated executive roles.

Strategic Leadership

President / Executive Director

Provides overall executive leadership for OMEF. The role includes:

  • Implementing the Foundation’s strategy across global and regional initiatives.
  • Representing OMEF in appropriate institutional, governmental, and public settings.
  • Developing and maintaining strategic relationships with high-level partners.
  • Supporting ongoing programme and institutional development.
  • Ensuring that Foundation activities remain aligned with its mission and governance framework.
Operational Coordination

Foundation Managing Director

Provides operational management and execution leadership. The role includes:

  • Supporting operational and technical execution across all delivery pathways.
  • Coordinating the day-to-day delivery of the Foundation’s programmatic work.
  • Overseeing internal management systems, cross-functional delivery, and administrative discipline.
  • Aligning regional hubs, field units, and partner deliverables with Foundation standards.
Section 7 · Practical Action

Programmes and Delivery

OMEF’s programmes and delivery activities translate its public-interest mission into practical, measurable action on the ground.

Ecological Restoration & Landscape Recovery

Restoring degraded watershed corridors, soil systems, and indigenous forest canopies, including the flagship Tano River restoration initiative.

Climate Resilience

Equipping vulnerable communities with adaptation infrastructure, water security buffers, and natural protections against extreme climate impacts.

Community Development & Livelihoods

Fostering sustainable, nature-positive local enterprise, regenerative agriculture, and community-owned stewardship mechanisms.

Education & Digital Capability

Building digital foundations, youth AI literacy, and practical skills through initiatives like the Asanteman Digital Future Pledge.

Research & Knowledge

Generating open scientific methodologies, peer-reviewed baselines, empirical tracking, and accessible public knowledge assets.

Institutional Strengthening

Enhancing local capacity, traditional governance integration, policy dialogue, and public convening leading into Africa Climate Week 2027 Kumasi.

Delivery Modality: Programmes may be delivered directly, through local teams, or through partnerships and other appropriate institutional arrangements.

Local & International Delivery

OMEF’s work is organised through local programmes, project teams, hubs, and international partnerships.

  • Convening Hub: Kumasi, Ghana serves as our primary operational convening center.
  • Ecosystem Evolution: The precise structure develops over time according to programmes, geographic reach, and operational requirements.

Section 11 · Public Trust

Accountability Framework

OMEF seeks to maintain clear lines of accountability between governance, executive leadership, and programme delivery.

Strategic Oversight

Continuous Board alignment ensuring that all activities adhere to our public-interest purpose.

Responsible Stewardship

Prudent financial allocation, independent audits, and strict resource safeguarding.

Programme & Partner Oversight

Comprehensive due diligence, co-designed milestones, and institutional review gates.

Financial & Operational Controls

Segregation of duties, multi-tier authorization, and robust risk management mechanisms.

Monitoring & Learning

Rigorous empirical feedback loops, data verification labels, and adaptive project management.

Transparent Reporting

Public disclosure of results, publications, and verified impact indicators.

Further information concerning OMEF’s leadership, programmes, impact, and organisational development will be published as appropriate.

Section 12 · Institutional Protection

What This Public Document Does Not Disclose

For reasons of confidentiality, security, legal privilege, and responsible organisational management, this public document does not set out:

  • Confidential Board or management procedures
  • Internal voting or approval mechanisms
  • Individual signing authorities
  • Detailed financial and banking controls
  • Confidential contractual arrangements
  • Internal personnel structures and records
  • Confidential partner agreements
  • Non-public operational safeguarding data
These matters are governed strictly by the Foundation’s constitutional and operational documents and by applicable law.
Section 13 · Dynamic Growth

Evolution of the Organisation

OMEF is a developing international foundation. Its organisational structure may evolve as its programmes, partnerships, and geographic activities develop.

Changes to the operational structure will remain subject to the Foundation’s governing framework and its continuing commitment to mission, integrity, accountability, and public benefit.

Our Mother Earth Foundation (OMEF)

Governance & Organisational Structure

People. Nature. Future.

1. Purpose of this Document

This document provides a high-level description of the governance and organisational architecture of Our Mother Earth Foundation (“OMEF”).

Its purpose is to help communities, partners, supporters, institutions and members of the public understand how the Foundation is structured and how responsibility is broadly allocated.

It is a public-facing overview. It does not reproduce OMEF's detailed constitutional, operational, financial or internal governance arrangements.

2. Governance Principles

OMEF's governance is designed to protect the Foundation's public-interest mission and to support responsible, transparent and effective delivery. The Foundation seeks to operate according to the following principles:

  • Mission protection — decisions should support the Foundation's public-interest purpose.
  • Responsible stewardship — the Foundation's resources should be managed responsibly and for their intended purposes.
  • Clear accountability — responsibilities should be allocated clearly between governance and executive functions.
  • Partnership — OMEF works collaboratively with communities and institutional, public, academic, philanthropic and private-sector partners.
  • Integrity and transparency — OMEF seeks to communicate openly about its work, impact and stewardship, subject to legitimate confidentiality and legal requirements.
  • Long-term thinking — governance should support initiatives capable of creating lasting benefit beyond individual projects.
3. Public Governance Architecture

OMEF's public governance architecture can be understood as follows:

BOARD OF DIRECTORS: Strategic oversight, stewardship and protection of the Foundation's mission
↓
EXECUTIVE LEADERSHIP: Implementation of strategy and organisational delivery
↓
PROGRAMMES & DELIVERY: Environmental restoration, climate resilience, community development, education, digital and related initiatives
PARTNERSHIPS & INSTITUTIONAL RELATIONS: Communities, public institutions, traditional leadership, academia, philanthropy, enterprise and other partners
RESEARCH, KNOWLEDGE & INNOVATION: Research, methodologies, learning, technology and knowledge-sharing
FINANCE, OPERATIONS & ADMINISTRATION: Financial stewardship, administration, operational support and organisational systems
LOCAL & INTERNATIONAL HUBS / PROJECT TEAMS: Local delivery and collaboration across the places in which OMEF works

4. The Board

The Board provides overall governance and strategic oversight of OMEF. Its role includes, at a high level: protecting the Foundation's mission and public-interest purpose; overseeing strategy and organisational direction; overseeing responsible stewardship of the Foundation's resources; providing appropriate oversight of programmes, partnerships and institutional development; supporting accountability and transparency; and overseeing the performance of executive leadership.

The Board does not manage every operational aspect of the Foundation. Day-to-day implementation is delegated to executive leadership within the Foundation's governing framework.

5. The Chair

The Chair provides leadership to the Board and supports effective governance. The Chair helps ensure that Board discussions remain focused on the Foundation's mission, strategic responsibilities and public-interest purpose. The Chair does not replace the collective responsibilities of the Board or the executive functions of management.

6. Executive Leadership

The President / Executive Director provides overall executive leadership for OMEF. The role includes: implementing the Foundation's strategy; representing OMEF in appropriate institutional and public settings; developing and maintaining strategic relationships; supporting programme and organisational development; and ensuring that the Foundation's activities remain aligned with its mission and governance framework.

The Foundation Managing Director supports operational and technical execution and coordinates the delivery of the Foundation's work.

7. Programmes and Delivery

OMEF's programmes and delivery activities translate its mission into practical action. Depending on the programme, this may include: ecological restoration and landscape recovery; climate resilience; community development and sustainable livelihoods; education and digital capability; research and knowledge; institutional strengthening; and other initiatives consistent with OMEF's public-interest mission.

Programmes may be delivered directly, through local teams, or through partnerships and other appropriate arrangements.

8. Partnerships

Partnership is central to OMEF's model. The Foundation may work with: communities and community organisations; public authorities and institutions; traditional leadership; universities and research institutions; philanthropic organisations; responsible businesses and enterprises; civil society organisations; international organisations and networks; and other organisations whose activities are consistent with OMEF's mission.

OMEF seeks to build partnerships around shared outcomes, local knowledge and responsible delivery.

9. Supporting Functions

The Foundation maintains appropriate professional, administrative and specialist functions to support its responsible operation, governance and delivery. These functions may include finance, administration, legal and compliance support, communications, technology, research, monitoring and evaluation, and other specialist expertise appropriate to the Foundation's activities. Some functions may be provided internally and others through appropriately appointed professional advisers, partners or service providers.

10. Local and International Delivery

OMEF's work may be organised through local programmes, project teams, hubs and international partnerships. The precise structure may develop over time according to the Foundation's programmes, geographic reach and operational requirements. Local delivery is intended to remain connected to the Foundation's overall mission, standards and governance framework.

11. Accountability

OMEF seeks to maintain clear lines of accountability between governance, executive leadership and programme delivery. The Foundation's governance framework supports: strategic oversight; responsible stewardship; programme and partnership oversight; appropriate financial and operational controls; monitoring and learning; and transparent reporting.

12. What This Public Document Does Not Disclose

For reasons of confidentiality, security, legal privilege and responsible organisational management, this public document does not set out: confidential Board or management procedures; internal voting or approval mechanisms; individual signing authorities; detailed financial controls; confidential contractual arrangements; internal personnel structures; confidential partner arrangements; or other information whose publication would not be appropriate or necessary for public understanding of OMEF.

These matters are governed by the Foundation's constitutional and operational documents and by applicable law.

13. Evolution of the Organisation

OMEF is a developing international foundation. Its organisational structure may evolve as its programmes, partnerships and geographic activities develop. Changes to the operational structure will remain subject to the Foundation's governing framework and its continuing commitment to mission, integrity, accountability and public benefit.
